Our customers are engineers who get woken up by alerts. Too many of those alerts are noise, and too many real problems are found by customers first. We want to help them get woken up less, and you'll be the one explaining how.

Kestrel checks websites and APIs from twelve regions every thirty seconds, and runs the status pages customers read when things break. We're a team of twenty-two, mostly engineers. This is our first developer advocate role, and you'll report to our CEO, who used to do it badly.

## What you'll do

- Write guides, examples and the occasional honest blog post, including about our own outages.
- Build sample projects and integrations: Terraform, GitHub Actions, the tools our customers already use.
- Give talks and run workshops, a few a year, never more than one a month.
- Answer questions where developers ask them, and turn the good ones into documentation.
- Bring what developers tell you back to the product team, with enough detail to act on.

## About you

- You've built software, run it in production and been on call for it.
- You like explaining things, in writing and out loud.
- Your writing or talks are public, and we can read or watch them.
- You'd rather show how something works than say it's great.

## Nice to have

- You've written documentation as well as blog posts.
- Video: short screencasts, not influencer content.

## How we work

Remote anywhere. We write first, meet when it helps, and keep Fridays free of meetings. You'll set your own plan each quarter, with a few goals we agree together.

## What we offer

- A travel budget for events, business class on flights over eight hours.
- 30 days off, plus public holidays where you live.
- A home-office budget, and the laptop of your choice.
- No targets on followers, views or sign-ups.

## How we hire

1. A call with our CEO.
2. A 20-minute talk or a written piece on a topic of your choice, to us and a few engineers.
3. A conversation with the product team.

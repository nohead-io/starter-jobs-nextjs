When our customers have an outage, they open Kestrel. They check what's failing, and their status page tells their own customers what's going on. So Kestrel can't have an outage at the same time, and big outages are exactly when our traffic is highest. You'll help make sure we stay up when everyone else is down.

Our checkers run in twelve regions, on three cloud providers, every thirty seconds. Our reliability team is three engineers in the Americas and two in Europe, so that someone is always awake.

## What you'll do

- Run our checkers in twelve regions and the pipeline behind them.
- Make status pages stay up under the traffic of a big outage, when a page can go from ten visits a minute to fifty thousand.
- Own our incident process, and write the reviews afterwards, without blame.
- Make sure we're never all on one provider, one region or one DNS.
- Take your turn on call, one week in five, during your daytime where we can.

## About you

- Years of running distributed systems, and some stories about it.
- Go or Rust, Linux, networking. Calm on call.
- You think about how things fail before you build them.
- You write clear incident reviews, and you read other people's for fun.

## Nice to have

- Experience with DNS, CDNs or anycast.
- You've run infrastructure across more than one cloud provider.

## How we work

Remote in North or South America. We write first, meet when it helps, and keep Fridays free of meetings. Every major incident gets a public review on our blog.

## What we offer

- A generous on-call allowance.
- A quiet week after every major incident: no tickets, no meetings.
- 30 days off, plus public holidays where you live.
- Health insurance, or a stipend where that makes more sense.

## How we hire

1. A call with our head of reliability.
2. A design conversation: keeping a status page up while half the internet is down.
3. A conversation about an incident you were part of, with two engineers.

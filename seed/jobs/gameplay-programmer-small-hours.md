Our games are calm, but they're not simple. Our last one, about keeping a lighthouse, ran on a dozen small systems: weather, light, supply boats and a cat that slept wherever it was least convenient. Our next one is about a small island ferry: timetables, tides, weather and passengers with their own plans.

Small Hours is a studio of fourteen. We make one game at a time, take about three years over each, and look after the last one while we make the next. You'll join two other programmers, working closely with our two designers.

## What you'll do

- Build gameplay systems in Unity and C#: the tides, the timetable, and passengers who miss the boat.
- Prototype quickly with the designers, then make what works solid.
- Care about how things feel: a door, a rope, a boat against a pier.
- Keep the game running well on the Switch and older laptops.
- Fix the bugs players find in the lighthouse game, when they find them.

## About you

- You've shipped a game, at a studio or on your own.
- Solid C#; you like systems that interact in surprising ways.
- You'd rather tune something for a day than add a new feature.
- You enjoy working closely with designers and artists.

## Nice to have

- Experience with console development.
- Simulation of any kind: weather, traffic, ecosystems.

## How we work

Remote in Europe, with a short call each morning and a playtest every Friday that everyone joins. Twice a year we meet for a week, usually somewhere with a ferry.

## What we offer

- No crunch. We mean it: we've moved release dates instead, twice.
- 30 days off, plus a week between Christmas and New Year.
- A share of each game's profits, for everyone at the studio.
- A copy of every game we make, and a budget for the games you play.

## How we hire

1. A call with our technical director about your past work.
2. A paid two-day prototype of a small system, on a brief we send you.
3. A playtest of what you made, with the team.

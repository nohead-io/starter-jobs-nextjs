The ferry game's interface is mostly paper: timetables pinned in the wheelhouse, tickets, hand-drawn maps of the islands and the captain's log. Players should feel they could fold it up and put it in a pocket. We need someone to draw it.

Small Hours is a studio of fourteen, making quiet games for the evening. Our last game, about keeping a lighthouse, sold half a million copies. This contract covers the ferry game's whole interface, alongside our art director and a programmer who will get it into the game.

## What you'll do

- Design and draw the game's interface, from menus to the log book.
- Draw the island maps and the timetables, in a style that matches the game's art.
- Make it all readable on a television across the room and on a Switch in your hands.
- Work with a programmer to get it into the game, and adjust what doesn't work once it's there.
- Prepare the interface for translation into nine languages, with room for longer words.

## About you

- A portfolio of game or illustrated interface work.
- Comfortable with Figma and an illustration tool of your choice.
- You care about type, and you've drawn letters by hand.
- You work well from a brief, and you ask questions when it's unclear.

## Nice to have

- Experience with interfaces for consoles.
- Hand lettering, maps or printmaking.

## How we work

Remote anywhere, with a weekly review with our art director and a playtest every Friday that you're welcome to join.

## What we offer

- A four-month contract, at the yearly rate above, paid monthly.
- No crunch: the schedule has room in it.
- Your name in the credits, and a copy of the game.

## How we hire

1. A call with our art director.
2. A portfolio walk-through.
3. A paid two-day sketch of one screen, on a brief we send you.

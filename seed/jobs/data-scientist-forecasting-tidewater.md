Our forecasts start from public weather models and get corrected for each harbour with what its own sensors measured. A national wave model might be right on average along a coast, and wrong by a metre at the entrance to one harbour. Those corrections are what our customers pay for, and you'll make them better.

Tidewater has a forecasting team of three: two data scientists and an oceanographer. Our customers are harbours, ferry operators and sailing apps, and some of them decide whether a ferry sails from what we tell them.

## What you'll do

- Build and test models that correct wave and wind forecasts locally, from buoys, tide gauges and harbour sensors.
- Decide, with numbers, when a new model is good enough to ship, and how we'd know if it got worse.
- Find out why a forecast was wrong, and whether the sensor, the model or the sea was to blame.
- Work with platform engineering to run new models every hour without anyone noticing.
- Explain forecast errors to customers who depend on them, honestly and in their terms.

## About you

- Experience with time series and physical data; Python and its scientific tools.
- You're careful with evaluation, and wary of a model that looks too good.
- You write clearly about uncertainty, for people who need to make a decision.
- An interest in the sea helps. Seasickness is allowed.

## Nice to have

- A background in oceanography, meteorology or another physical science.
- Experience running models in production, not only in notebooks.

## How we work

Remote anywhere, with four hours of overlap with UTC. Most of our decisions are made in documents, and the forecasting team reviews each other's experiments every week.

## What we offer

- A flexible schedule around those four hours.
- 28 days off, plus public holidays where you live.
- A conference of your choice each year, and time to write a paper if you have one in you.
- One sailing trip a year with the team, which is research.

## How we hire

1. A call with our head of forecasting.
2. A conversation about a past project of yours, in depth.
3. A paid half-day exercise on a real harbour's data, and a talk about it with the team.
